# Ogma Typing privacy policy

Last updated: November 9, 2026

Ogma Typing is a Windows utility published by Raul Pop for inserting emoji and saved text shortcuts.

## Keyboard and accessibility access

While enabled, Ogma receives keyboard events to detect emoji queries and text-shortcut triggers. It keeps only the current bounded trigger query in memory; it does not save a history of what you type. It uses Windows accessibility information to identify the focused field, check for protected/password fields, and position the picker beside the caret. It does not read document text through accessibility APIs. Disable Ogma from its notification-area menu to stop its keyboard listener.

## Information stored on your PC

Ogma stores the text shortcuts you create, app preferences, and emoji-use counts/recency locally. Shortcuts may contain personal information you choose to save. Ogma uses this data to offer your saved shortcuts and recent/frequent emoji. The app does not use the clipboard to insert text.

The local data location depends on whether you use the portable app or Microsoft Store package. The portable app uses the Ogma folder in your local application-data directory. Windows can manage or redirect app data for the Store package. You can delete saved shortcuts in Settings. No cross-device sync is provided.

## Network access and sharing

Ogma does not upload typed text, saved shortcuts, emoji history, or app preferences. Search works offline. It contains no advertising or analytics service. The View releases command opens GitHub in your browser; GitHub and your browser apply their own privacy policies. Windows and Microsoft Store may collect diagnostics or account information under your Microsoft/Windows settings and Microsoft's policies, independently of Ogma.

## Contact and changes

For privacy questions, contact the publisher through the [Ogma project](https://github.com/raulpop8/ogma/issues). Do not post passwords or private shortcut contents in a public issue. This policy will be updated if the app's data practices change.
